Italy Region Fixed Effects vs. Rugged Score, Precipitation & Motorcycles per Capita Scatterplot Matrix

This scatterplot matrix visualises the relationships between region fixed effects from a multivariate regression in my project against three features: topography rugged score, annual precipitation, and motorcycles per capita. Each subplot shows the scatterplot between two variables, along with a linear regression line to highlight trends. Histograms along the diagonal display the distribution of each individual variable. I chose a scatterplot matrix to effectively visualise potential correlations across multiple variables (more than two) simultaneously.

Big Data. Extracting a story from millions of prices (see instructions).

Wine Price Distribution by Type: AutoCPI data. Davies and McElvoy (2024).

I plotted the distribution of wine prices by type using data from the AutoCPI dataset. Since the distributions had a similar shape and covered similar price ranges, I chose to stack them when plotting them together. I added drop down menus to allow users to select the wine type (including all types) and the store (including all stores). To make it easier to visualise, I chose colours that corresponded to the wine types. Finally, I added a box plot on top, summarising key statistics for each distribution.

You can view the Jupyter Notebook on my GitHub: html (better for viewing) | notebook.

Wine Price History: Long Run Prices Database (LRPD). Davies (2021)

I plotted the history of wine prices by type using data from the Long Run Prices Database (LRPD). I chose to visualise key quantiles of the price distribution over time for each wine type. I avoided plotting mins and maxs because of the presence of outliers in the data, particularly on the maxs side. As before, to make it easier to visualise, I chose colours that corresponded to the wine types. Finally, I added a dropdown menu to allow users to select the wine type.

Debate. Use a visualisation in policy commentary (see instructions).

EU-27 CO2 Emissions and Targets

Is the EU on track to meet its emissions targets? In this chart, we visualise the EU-27's CO2 emissions over time, highlighting the key targets set for 2030 (55% reduction of level in 1990) and 2050 (net zero). I project the 2004-2024 trend (we see consistent declines in emissions over that period) all the way to 2050. This graph shows that, if the trend continues, the EU may come close to meeting the 2030 target, but will likely fall considerably short of the 2050 net zero goal. This means that policies must be tightened, or targets need to be adjusted.

You can view the Jupyter Notebook on my GitHub: html (better for viewing) | notebook.

EU-27 CO2 Emissions by Source

Where should the EU focus its efforts to reduce emissions? This chart breaks down the EU-27's CO2 emissions by source, showing that if present trends continue, the only emissions source that will reach net zero by 2050 is coal, which would reach it by 2041. Other sources would take much longer, beyond the 2050 target year: cement in 2061, oil in 2085, other industry in 2087, gas in 2118, and flaring in 2125.
