Report: Road Accidents with Injuries in Italy

Aims

The purpose of this study is to analyse the trends and patterns of road accidents with injuries in Italy from 2010 to 2024, with a focus on understanding the drivers of differences across regions and time. I start with the chart below, which shows the number of casualties and the number of casualties per 100,000 inhabitants over time. The trend is largely consistent across regions, steady decline until 2019, then a sharp drop in 2020 due to the COVID-19 pandemic, followed by an increase in 2021 and 2022 as economic activity and mobility resumed, but still lower than pre-pandemic levels. In 2024, the total number of casualties (injured + killed) amounted to 401.7 people per 100,000 inhabitants, up from 385.90 in 2023.

Jupyter Notebook on my GitHub: html (better for viewing) | notebook.

To begin to understand these trends, it is important to consider the regional heterogeneity in road accidents with injuries. The map below shows several metrics related to road accidents with injuries in Italy by region for the studied period. Ratios such as the number of accidents per 100,000 inhabitants, or the mortality rate allow us to compare regions of different sizes and populations. We can see that some regions have consistently higher rates of accidents and casualties than others, indicating potential structural issues such as road infrastructure. Liguria stands out with the highest accident rate, although its mortality rate is not the highest.

Jupyter Notebook on my GitHub: html (better for viewing) | notebook.

The chart below breaks down accident occurrence by the time in which it occurs: month of the year, day of the week, and hour of the day. Looking at the trends by month of the year, we can see a slight uptick in accidents during the summer months. Looking at days of the week, we see a clear decline in the accident rates on the weekend. Finally, looking at hours of the day, we see that there is a lower accident rate late at night and in the early morning, but they peak at 8:00 and 18:00 (likely, rush hour).

Data

The main source for the data is IstatData (a service of the Italian National Statistics Institute). Unfortunately, I did not find an API, and I was not able to scrape the data tables. I had to manually download the CSV files I needed. As we will see later, I also worked with topography data (obtained from an online database for elevation tiles) and weather data (obtained through the Open-Meteo API). The Open-Meteo API does not require a key, but it seems to be very "strict" and it appears that it does not permit frequent queries.

We clearly see that variables such as commutes shorter than 15 minutes, government expenditure in public order, government expenditure in education, government expenditure in social protection have a significant negative effect on the number of accidents per 100,000 inhabitants. On the other hand, variables such as the proportion of people who commute by motorcycle or moped and GDP per capita have a significant positive effect on the number of accidents per 100,000 inhabitants.

Let's turn now to the fixed effects:

Jupyter Notebook on my GitHub: html (better for viewing) | notebook.

We clearly see that Liguria, for example, has a considerable, significant positive fixed effect. This suggests that there are structural factors in Liguria that lead to a higher number of accidents with injuries per 100,000 inhabitants. I thus looked at topography (rugged index), weather (precipitation), and vehicle register (in particular, motorcycles). These variables likely do not vary much over time within regions. The scatterplot matrix below shows the relationships between these variables and the fixed effects:

Jupyter Notebook on my GitHub: html (better for viewing) | notebook.

In the third plot in the final row, we can see a clear positive relationship between motorcycle density and fixed effects. This explains part of the high fixed effect in Liguria, which has a high motorcycle density.

Finally, I ran a PCA / cluster analysis to group cities based on the accident conditions / circumstances they experience as well as their vehicle registry. This would be useful when tailoring solutions and implementing them across all the cities. Conclusions

In this report, I analysed road accidents with injuries in Italy from 2010 to 2024. I found that there has been a general decline in accidents and casualties over time, with a sharp drop in 2020 due to the COVID-19 pandemic. However, there are significant regional differences in accident rates, with some regions consistently experiencing higher rates than others. Through regression analysis, I identified several factors that influence accident rates, including commute patterns and government expenditure. Fixed effects analysis highlighted structural issues in certain regions, such as Liguria, which can be partially explained by factors like motorcycle density. Finally, cluster analysis provided insights into grouping municipalities based on accident conditions, which can inform targeted interventions. Overall, this study provides a comprehensive understanding of road accidents with injuries in Italy and highlights areas for further investigation and policy action.
